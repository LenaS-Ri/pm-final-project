# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Role: Lena, 32, Cologne, is a salaried mobile-first shopper with limited financial cushion who regularly uses BNPL for non-trivial purchases and currently switches between Klarna, PayPal and Riverty depending on what is available at checkout.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Goal 1: She wants to stay in control of when her money leaves her account, particularly while she is still deciding what to keep or return.
Goal 2: She wants one clear view of what she owes, when it is due, and what requires her attention, regardless of where she shopped.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Friction. Moment of Misery: She returns part of an order, but the invoice still shows the full amount and payment is approaching. She now has to decide whether to pay for something she returned or risk a late fee while waiting for the return to be processed. 
With several purchases outstanding, payment information is scattered. A reminder arrives while a return is unresolved, or the app and email show different due dates, leaving her unsure what she actually needs to pay and when.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Lena effectively has to become her own payment operations manager.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** 1. Checks multiple payment providers: Opens Klarna, PayPal and/or Riverty separately to understand which purchases sit where. This follows naturally from her behavior of using whichever provider is available at checkout. 

2. Cross-checks emails and app notifications:
Searches confirmation emails, reminders and app information to work out upcoming payment dates, especially when information conflicts.

3. Checks the merchant separately for returns:
Goes back to the merchant's app/site or return confirmation to verify whether a returned item has actually been received and refunded.

4. Manually reconciles return vs. payment status:
Compares the merchant's return status with the outstanding BNPL invoice. When they don't match, she has to decide whether to wait or pay anyway.

5. Creates her own reminders:
Likely relies on email flags, phone/calendar reminders, or repeatedly checking apps to avoid missing a payment. This specific behavior is inferred and should be validated in research.

6. Falls back to another payment method:
When Riverty isn't available or creates too much uncertainty, she can simply use PayPal, Klarna or her existing debit/credit card. The case explicitly identifies existing BNPL options and existing cards as the dominant status quo.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The core inefficiency isn't the number of clicks. It's that Lena has to reconstruct her financial position herself from systems that don't provide one consistent source of truth.
She has to answer three basic questions manually:
What do I owe? → When will I pay? → Has my return changed that?
That creates cognitive load and uncertainty precisely when BNPL is supposed to give her more control. The workaround also becomes increasingly fragile as the number of purchases, merchants and payment providers grows.
