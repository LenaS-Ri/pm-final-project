Slide 5 · Strategy

Why Riverty needs a card, and why now

Problem hook

Riverty risks losing relevance beyond existing merchants' checkouts, while consumers like Lena lack one flexible payment relationship that keeps them in control, especially when things don't go as planned.

Value proposition

The Riverty Card combines the broad acceptance of Mastercard with the flexibility of Riverty's BNPL experience, enabling customers to pay wherever Mastercard is accepted while staying in control of their finances.

By integrating card and checkout purchases into one clear overview in the existing Riverty app, customers like Lena can easily see what they owe, understand payment statuses and manage their purchases without switching between different payment experiences.

For Riverty, this creates a direct, everyday consumer relationship beyond merchant checkouts, driving repeat card usage and retention while strengthening its merchant-first ecosystem.

Data-backed hypothesis

Based on recurring qualitative evidence of fragmented payment visibility and return-related uncertainty, supported by a simulated baseline of 12.5 return-related payment enquiries per 1,000 transactions, I believe that giving Lena one broadly accepted payment relationship with flexible repayment and a unified payment overview will result in increased repeat Riverty usage, as measured by ≥50% of activated cardholders completing at least 2 Riverty Card transactions in Month 6. I will protect partner-merchant checkout conversion, allowing no more than a 1pp decline, and will make a go/no-go decision after 6 months with at least 1,000 activated cardholders.

Slide 6 · Research

Lena has to become her own payment operations manager

Workaround: the manual process

  • Checks multiple providers. Opens Klarna, PayPal and/or Riverty separately.
  • Cross-checks emails and notifications to work out upcoming payment dates.
  • Checks the merchant separately to verify a return was received and refunded.
  • Manually reconciles return status against the outstanding BNPL invoice.
  • Creates her own reminders (inferred, to be validated in research).
  • Falls back to another payment method such as PayPal, Klarna or her existing card.

Core frustration: she has to reconstruct her financial position herself from systems with no single source of truth: What do I owe? When will I pay? Has my return changed that?

Evidence

"That's actually the annoying part. Everything is scattered."INT-05 · moderate BNPL user managing several outstanding purchases
"Returned order 8 days ago but invoice still shows full amount. Do I need to pay this now? I don't want a late fee while waiting for the merchant."SUP-18421

Advantages vs. the workaround

  • Broad reach: one Riverty relationship across merchants and online/offline purchases.
  • Unified overview: replaces checking multiple providers, emails and reminders.
  • Integrated control: payment flexibility, visibility and issue handling in one experience.
1 · SHOP & PAYLena shops online or in-store and uses her Riverty Card wherever Mastercard is accepted, instead of depending on merchant checkout availability.
2 · CHECK PURCHASESShe opens the Riverty app to review her card and existing Riverty checkout purchases.
3 · MOMENT OF MISERYShe has returned an item but is unsure whether the refund has been processed, what she still owes or when payment is due. Previously, she pieced together information from different sources.
4 · SOLUTIONThe unified chronological overview shows card and checkout purchases together, with clear payment-source labels, amounts and statuses.
5 · OUTCOMELena finds the purchase, checks its recorded payment or refund status and understands what requires her attention.

Slide 7 · Blueprint

Unified Card + BNPL Overview: scope and PRD

Must

  • Unified transaction feed
  • Clear purchase-source labels: “Paid with Riverty” vs. “Paid with Riverty Card”
  • Core details: merchant, amount, date, status
  • Status handling: Pending, Completed, Refunded, Reversed
  • Refund/reversal linked to the original transaction
  • One chronological ordering
  • Transaction detail view

Should

  • Transaction filters
  • Merchant search
  • Attention-needed indicators
  • Card balance summary
  • Merchant-name enrichment
  • Contextual explanations

Could

  • Spending categories and monthly summaries
  • Merchant logos
  • Advanced search and filtering
  • Custom transaction tags
  • Transaction-history export
  • Personalised spending insights
  • Merchant location/map

Won't (now)

  • Flexible Card Repayment configuration
  • Apple Pay / Google Pay setup
  • Physical card management
  • Rewards or cashback
  • Premium card benefits
  • Dispute and chargeback workflows
  • Advanced budgeting
  • New credit decisioning or identity verification
  • Major app redesign
  • Automatic merchant-return reconciliation

PRD highlights

  • JTBD: “Help me stay in control of what I owe and when I pay, wherever I shop.”
  • Three screens: Riverty Overview, Card Transaction Detail, Refund Updated.
  • Safety guard: the UI never infers a return or refund from context. “Status unavailable” beats a plausible but fabricated explanation.
  • Scope rule: the prototype shows transaction state. It does not create or control it.
  • Now lane: Consumer Card Onboarding Journey, Virtual Card Provisioning.

Slide 8 · Validation

A/B experiment: grouped sections vs. one chronological feed

Hypothesis & metrics

Feature
Unified Card + BNPL Overview in Riverty App (2 variants)
Persona
Lena, 32, mobile-first BNPL user who wants control over purchases, returns and payment obligations
Expected outcome
Users find and understand purchases more easily in one chronological overview, reducing the need to contact customer service
Primary metric
Overview-related CS contacts per 1,000 invoice-equivalent transaction records (long-term: M6 Active Card Rate ≥50%)
Baseline
120 contacts per 1,000 invoices (simulated)
MDE
−20 contacts per 1,000 invoices (−16.7%)
Guardrails
Partner-merchant checkout conversion: no decline over 1pp vs. baseline/control. No increase in unresolved payment complaints or incorrect financial-status information
Design
4,500 invoices per arm · 50/50 split · 30 days or until sample reached · 95% confidence, two-sided, p < 0.05

Control (A)

Card and checkout purchases in the same overview, grouped into two sections: “Paid with Riverty” and “Paid with Riverty Card.”

Variant (B)

Card and checkout purchases together in one chronological feed, each with a “Paid with Riverty” or “Paid with Riverty Card” label.

Held constant

Identical data, amounts, dates, statuses, labels, details and refund information. The only difference is transaction organisation: grouped by payment method vs. one chronological feed.

ShipPrimary metric improves by ≥ −20 contacts per 1,000 invoices at 95% confidence and the merchant-conversion guardrail holds.
IterateDirection is positive but lift is below the MDE.
KillPrimary metric shows no improvement or moves negatively. Read date is fixed: no results reviewed before it.
